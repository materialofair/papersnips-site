How to Print Multiple Photos on One A4 Page from iPhone

To put several iPhone photos on one sheet, arrange them on an A4-sized page first, export that page as a PDF, then print the PDF. This gives you a layout to check before using paper and ink, without moving the photos into Word on a computer.

A guide from the PaperSnips developer. Product details checked against the public version 1.0.1 listing on 17 October 2026. These steps are based on published app information; no physical printer testing was performed for this guide.

On this page

  1. Plan the grid and photo sizes
  2. Create an A4 layout
  3. Check dimensions and cropping
  4. Export a PDF
  5. Check paper size and print

1. Decide whether you need a grid or a particular photo size

For a school project or a page of holiday snapshots, a simple grid may be enough. If you will cut the pictures out for a journal, measure the space where each print will go before choosing the layout.

A4 is 210 × 297 mm. The sheet size and each photo's size are separate settings. “Four photos per page” describes a layout; it does not automatically mean four prints of a particular width and height.

Leave room around the sheet's edges and between pictures. Your printer may have an unprintable border, and a small gap makes trimming easier. Use its manual for the required margins rather than assuming borderless printing is available.

2. Start an A4 layout and add your photos

In PaperSnips 1.0.1, start with the A4 workbench. You can choose a layout for 2, 4, 6 or 9 photos, or set custom rows and columns. Import the pictures you want from Photos or Files. The current App Store listing documents these options: PaperSnips on the App Store

Begin with a small batch so you can check the arrangement. Review the page preview instead of assuming the order or crop is right. If you need twelve pictures on one sheet, their dimensions and the available page area still need to fit; a higher count does not remove that constraint.

3. Set dimensions and inspect the crop

Set individual photo dimensions in millimetres or centimetres when physical size matters. Check both numbers: 8 × 6 cm is 80 × 60 mm. Keep the proportions if you want to preserve the original shape.

PaperSnips offers Fit to retain the whole image and Fill to crop it to the frame. Inspect faces, text and objects near the edges before exporting. Adjust the crop focus where needed. You can also move, copy or remove photos and undo or redo edits.

As a planning example, four 80 × 60 mm frames arranged in two columns and two rows, with 5 mm between frames, occupy 165 × 125 mm before outer margins. That fits comfortably within A4. This is a size calculation, not a promise about a preset's default dimensions.

If you see more pages than expected, check page count, sizes and spacing before export. If you want everything on one sheet, adjust the layout and inspect the preview again.

4. Export the finished layout as a PDF

For a complete print job, export all pages as a PDF. PaperSnips also exports the current page as a 300 dpi JPEG. Its support page explains the difference and the printing workflow: PaperSnips printing and export support

Save the PDF through the iOS share sheet, then open it to confirm the page count and arrangement. Print that exported layout rather than selecting the original photos again. Otherwise, you may be sending separate pictures to the printer instead of the page you prepared.

5. Match the paper and check scaling

Load A4 paper and select A4 in the printing app where that option is available. For AirPrint, Apple says the iPhone and an AirPrint-enabled printer must be on the same Wi-Fi network. Open the PDF in an app that supports printing and use its Share or actions menu to find Print. See Apple's printing guide: Apple’s guide to printing from iPhone

When exact dimensions matter, choose Actual Size or 100% if your printing app provides that control. “Fit” can resize the entire page. The labels and available options depend on the app and printer; not every iPhone print screen exposes a scaling setting. If yours does not, check the printer maker's app or ask your print service to print the PDF at its original size.

Print one test sheet before a larger batch. Measure one photo with a ruler and inspect the edges. If it is too small or cropped, check paper matching, scaling and printable margins before changing the layout. For a desktop printing example, Adobe explains the distinction between Fit and Actual Size: Adobe’s PDF page-size guidance
